Writ is an options desk built for income rather than leverage. A user sells a cash-secured put or a covered call and receives the premium immediately. Every position is collateralized at its full notional, so there are no liquidations, no margin calls and no pooled risk between writers. Expiry settles against the first Pyth price at or after the expiry timestamp, which no party can influence by choosing when to claim.

Physical settlement is the product

A cash-settled option pays a difference, which is not what the writer asked for. Someone who says "I would buy BTC at $74,000" and sees it finish at $70,000 gets a smaller balance from a cash-settled desk. Writ gives them BTC, at $74,000 — the price they named, plus the premium they were already paid.

At expiry an option is either out of the money, and every leg returns to where it came from, or assigned, and the two sides swap in full at the strike. There is no partial payout, because no difference is being settled.

A venue, not a house

  • Makers hold their own balances in the contract and sign their own quotes. The protocol matches and settles; it never takes a side.
  • Writing an option reserves the side that maker may owe, so no position can open that its counterparty could not honour at expiry.
  • A quote names its maker and is checked against that maker's key, so one maker's signer can never commit another's inventory.
  • A request for quote asks every registered maker at once, holds a short window open and returns the best bid. A maker that misses the window is skipped rather than closing the market.

Properties enforced by the contract

  • Solvency, per asset. Each token balance always equals makerFree + makerReserved + writerCollateral. Both are fuzz-tested.
  • Delivery is pre-funded. A position cannot be opened unless its counterparty already holds what it might owe, so assignment can never fail for lack of inventory.
  • A quote signer cannot move funds. It only sets premiums on its own maker's quotes.
  • Reserves are unreachable. A maker may withdraw only its uncommitted balance.
  • Settlement cannot be timed. parsePriceFeedUpdatesUnique pins the first tick after expiry, with confidence-interval and exponent guards.
  • The oracle sits behind an adapter. IOracleAdapter lets a second feed drop in without touching the core.
WHY GIWA

The same desk returns more premium on GIWA.

Writ runs on signed quotes. That design has exactly two weaknesses, and GIWA removes both at the architecture level rather than by convention.

Preconfirmation. A quote must survive until the writer's transaction lands. The longer it lives, the more stale-price risk the maker prices into the spread. Flashblocks preconfirm in roughly 200 ms, so our quotes live 20 seconds instead of 60: about three times less stale risk, and correspondingly more premium kept by the writer.

Private orderflow. There is no public mempool. Only the sequencer sees pending transactions, so a signed quote cannot be observed and traded against before it lands.

Counterparty model and honest status

Counterparties are market makers, not the protocol and not other users. Premiums are paid from a maker's own on-chain balance, and the contract refuses a trade that maker could not deliver on. At expiry only that position's own collateral and reserve move. Writer collateral is never pooled with another writer's.

On testnet the maker is operated by the team, which is stated plainly rather than dressed up as liquidity. The architecture is the multi-maker one either way: registering a second maker is three calls, and the request-for-quote flow already selects the best bid among however many answer.
